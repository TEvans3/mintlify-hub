Trial Sign-Up Response
Objective: redirect from product exploration to discovery conversation. They found you — they have a problem. Don't waste it on a feature tour.
⚡ The trap to avoid: treating a trial sign-up as a product demo request. Most reps send onboarding tips. You're going to send a commercial insight and propose a discovery call first.
Primary response — email (send within 2 hours of sign-up)
EmailPrimary response · Send within 2 hrs
Subject: Your Mintlify trial — a different starting point
Tone: Confident, curious, non-generic
[Name], Most people who sign up for a documentation platform trial want to check feature coverage. Before we go down that road — I'd rather spend 10 minutes understanding what you're actually trying to solve. The teams who get the most out of Mintlify usually aren't solving a documentation quality problem. They're solving a documentation workflow problem — the gap between what ships and what's documented is structural, and a better UI alone doesn't close it. What does is changing how documentation integrates with the engineering workflow. [PERSONALIZATION — pick one based on your research:] → I looked at [Company]'s developer docs — the update timestamps suggest a gap between your shipping pace and what's documented. → [Company] is hiring for [Technical Writer / DevRel] — which usually signals the current approach isn't scaling with the product. → [Company] has a developer-facing API product. That's exactly where documentation workflow failures show up as developer adoption problems. If you're open to it: rather than starting with a standard product walkthrough, could we do a 20-minute conversation first about what's driving the trial? I'll make sure whatever we show you is mapped to your actual situation — not a general feature tour. What does your calendar look like this week? Tony Evans | Mintlify
🎯 PERSONALIZATION REQUIRED: The bracket above is mandatory, not optional. Send this without a specific observation about their company and it reads like a mass email. Check their public docs, GitHub activity, and job postings before sending — takes 10 minutes, makes the difference between a reply and an archive.
If they reply saying they just want to explore the product themselves: respect it. Say "Completely understood — here's the best way to get value from the trial: [specific recommendation]. If questions come up, I'm here." Then set a 5-day re-engage if no further activity.

Demo Request Response
Objective: reframe from "show me the product" to "let's understand your problem first." The demo is more valuable after discovery. Propose a discovery-first structure.
⚡ The trap: booking a 45-minute demo and spending 40 minutes clicking through the product. You end up with someone who says "looks good, I'll share with my team" and you never hear from them again. The discovery call first gives you the pain, the persona, and the internal path before you ever open the product.

Referral / Warm Intro
Objective: leverage the warm signal without wasting it on pleasantries. A referral is credibility borrowed — use it in the first sentence, then immediately go to commercial insight. Don't spend three paragraphs thanking people.
⚡ The trap: "I was referred by [Name] who thought you might be interested in Mintlify..." and then a product pitch. You've just converted a warm intro into a cold email. Lead with why the referral is relevant — the specific problem you think they have.

Re-Engage — Warm Lead Gone Cold (30+ Days)
Objective: reopen the conversation with new value — not a status check. The cardinal rule: never "just checking in." Bring a trigger, a proof point, or a new angle that's relevant to something you've learned since the last conversation.
⚡ The three legitimate reasons to re-engage: (1) something changed at their company — new hire, job posting, product launch, funding; (2) you have something genuinely new to share — a relevant proof point, a piece of research, a competitive development; (3) enough time has passed that the previous conversation is stale and it's worth restarting fresh. "Checking in" is not one of them.

Re-engage — honest close (if 2+ previous re-engages have gone unanswered)

[Name], I'll keep this short. I've reached out a few times since our initial conversation and haven't heard back — which usually means one of three things: timing is genuinely off, the problem isn't a priority right now, or I've been reaching out at the wrong moment. This email has a 40–50% response rate even after long silences. It works because it's honest, gives them three face-saving exits, and asks one genuine question. The responses are almost always useful — either they re-engage or they tell you exactly why not, which saves you time.
