How we work: You book the meetings. I run the deals. But we are not siloed — the best pipeline we'll create comes from coordinated outreach into the same account from different angles at the same time. This guide tells you exactly how we do that.
The partnership model
🔵 Tony (AE) owns
Accounts with existing inbound connections or senior relationships
Enterprise accounts where first touch needs to be senior (Bloomberg, Broadridge)
All meetings from first call forward — discovery, champion building, EB meetings
Multi-thread coordination — I tell you who to reach and when
Account strategy — angle, proof point, Why Now
Pre-call plan for every meeting you book
🟢 You (SDR) own
Cold outreach on all SDR-led accounts — email, call, LinkedIn
Sequence execution — hitting every touch on cadence
First reply handling — respond, qualify, book the meeting
Multi-thread contacts — when I activate you, you own that thread
Research — confirm trigger, find right contact, verify doc tool before outreach
Cadence log — every touch logged in the sheet the day it happens
The three operating modes
🟢
SDR-Led
You own primary outreach. Cold account, no existing relationship. You run the full sequence until you book or exhaust the cadence. I review your messaging before you send and brief you on the account angle.
🔵
AE-Led
I own primary outreach — inbound signal, existing relationship, or senior enterprise account. You support by researching additional contacts and standing by to multi-thread if I ask.
🟡
Joint / Multi-thread
We're both active in the account — different contacts, coordinated angles. I handle Champion/EB track. You handle a second contact independently. We do NOT cross-reference each other until I say so.
The non-negotiable rules
✓
Never reach out to an account without checking with me first. Surprise outreach to an account I'm already working creates chaos. Check the Territory Command sheet before starting any sequence.
✓
Log every touch the day it happens. If it's not in the SDR Cadence Log, it doesn't exist. I use that log to brief myself before every call you generate.
✓
When someone replies — loop me in within the hour. Don't let a warm reply sit. Text me, Slack me, forward it. I'll tell you how to respond or take it from there.
✓
In joint-mode accounts — do NOT mention each other. We look like independent signals. The moment you say "my AE is also talking to someone there," the coordinated strategy is compromised.
✓
Brief me before you hand off a meeting. Send me the account, contact, what was said in the sequence, and the trigger. I need 10 minutes of context before I open my mouth on that call.
✓
Weekly sync is mandatory — 30 minutes, same time every week. Everything non-urgent waits for the sync. Don't Slack me 12 questions across the week when 11 can be answered in 30 minutes on Thursday.
The sheet is our shared source of truth. If you don't know what to do with an account — check the sheet. If I don't know what you've been doing in an account — I check the sheet. Keep it current and it makes both our jobs easier.
The 3 tabs — what each one is for
1
Territory Command (green tab)
Your main view. One row per account. Shows who owns outreach (AE-Led / SDR-Led / Joint / Unworked), current stage, meeting status, multi-thread flag, compelling event, and notes. Review together in weekly sync. Update the Stage and Outreach Owner columns every time something changes — that's all this tab needs from you. Your Outreach tool handles activity logging.
2
Meeting Handoffs (purple tab)
Every meeting you book gets a row here. You fill in the left side (account, contact, trigger, their verbatim reply) when you book. Tony fills in the right side (call outcome, multi-thread instruction, your next action) within 24 hours of the call. Check it — don't wait to be told.
3
Weekly Sync (orange tab)
Fill in your sections before our Thursday call — what's working in outreach, what isn't, accounts you want Tony's direction on. Tony fills in his sections — priorities for the week, multi-thread activations, accounts to deprioritize. We go through it together. 30-minute hard stop.
Your daily workflow with the sheet
AM

MID

Don't wait until the day before the call. Fill in the handoff row the same day you book. Send Tony the brief via Slack using the template in the Handoff Protocol tab.
Our buyer is an engineering leader, not a business buyer. You are reaching engineers who are already dealing with the problem you're going to describe. Lead with insight, not a pitch.
Primary target personas
🎯 Tier 1 SDR target — Head of DevRel / Developer Experience
Highest-conversion cold contact at Mintlify accounts. They own the documentation pain personally and feel it every day. They respond to the commercial insight because it articulates something they've been living with. Caution: they often don't have budget authority. Your job is to book the meeting — my job is to qualify whether they're a Mobilizer or Talker.
🎯 Tier 1 SDR target — VP of Engineering / CTO
The Economic Buyer. Higher bar for cold outreach but highest payoff. Lead with the engineering tax framing — they care about engineering throughput, not documentation aesthetics. The Coinbase proof point (20 min → 60 sec) is your opener. Keep cold outreach short and specific to their company.
🟡 Multi-thread target — Staff / Senior Engineer or Engineering Manager
Not your primary cold target but excellent for multi-threading inside an active account. They live with documentation pain daily and can become internal mobilizers. Use when I have the DevRel/VP track and need a second thread from a different angle.
Top verticals — in priority order
1. FinTech Highest urgency
Their product is the API. Documentation staleness directly impacts developer adoption. Proof point: Coinbase. NYC accounts: Brex, Plaid, Nuvei, Broadridge.
"When a developer hits a stale doc mid-integration, they don't file a ticket — they evaluate your competitor's API."
2. AI / ML Fastest close
Their AI strategy depends on AI-readable documentation. Proof point: Anthropic (co-developed LLMs.txt standard).
"If your docs aren't AI-native, they're invisible to the channel developers increasingly use to find answers."
3. Developer Tools
Their docs are their product. Documentation friction is adoption friction is revenue friction. Proof point: Coinbase + Layers (40–60 hrs/month saved).
"A developer hitting a dead end in your docs evaluates whether your tool is worth integrating."
4. SaaS / PLG
Self-serve motion depends on documentation quality. Every dead end is a support ticket or churned trial. Proof point: HubSpot (50% reduction in eng resources).
"Every docs dead-end in your self-serve flow is a support ticket or a churned trial you paid to acquire."
Pre-outreach research checklist — 10 min per account
✓
Visit their developer docs — scroll to the footer to identify the current platform. Note whether a public developer portal exists at all.
✓
Check for visible staleness — compare doc timestamps to their GitHub release history. A 30+ day gap is a personalization hook.
✓
LinkedIn: new VP Eng / CTO hires — filter "changed jobs in past 90 days." New engineering leadership is your best outreach window.
✓
LinkedIn Jobs: documentation / DevRel hiring — search for Technical Writer, DevRel, Developer Experience posted in last 60 days. That's the commercial insight in real time.
✓
Crunchbase / press — recent funding, product launch, or AI announcement. One specific event turns generic email into relevant email.
✓
Check with Tony first — before starting any sequence, confirm I haven't already reached this account or contact. Takes 30 seconds. Saves massive awkwardness.

The standard handoff protocol
1
Book the meeting — immediately fill in the Meeting Handoffs tab
Don't wait until the day before. Fill it in when you book: account, contact, title, what was said, what triggered the reply, and the meeting date. Add Tony as a calendar co-host.
2
Send Tony a Slack/text brief — same day as booking
Use the template below. Copy their exact reply verbatim — don't paraphrase. Their specific words are the qualification data.
3
Confirm Tony has seen it 48 hours before the call
If you haven't seen "AE Briefed = Yes" in the handoff tab 48 hours before — follow up. Don't let a cold-booked meeting happen without Tony being prepared. A bad first call reflects on both of us.
4
Post-call: check the handoff tab for your next action
Within 24 hours of the call, Tony updates the outcome and whether multi-threading is needed. Check the tab — don't wait to be told what to do next.
Handoff brief template — copy this format every time
📅 Meeting booked: [Contact name, title] at [Company] — [Date + time]
📧 Replied to: [Email step X / LinkedIn DM / Call on Day X]
💬 Their exact words: "[paste their reply verbatim]"
🔧 Current doc tool: [Confluence / GitBook / ReadMe / etc.]
⚡ Trigger / Why Now: [New VP Eng hire / job posting / product launch / etc.]
🎯 ICP tier: T1 / T2
❓ Anything I'm not sure about: [Qualification gaps Tony should probe in the call]

The ramp timeline
Week 1–2
Learn the territory. Score accounts. Understand the ICP. Don't send anything without Tony reviewing it first. First outreach goes out end of week 2 after Tony has approved your first sequence draft.
Week 3–4
First sequences running on Tier 1 accounts. Weekly syncs calibrating messaging. Route every positive reply to Tony immediately. Target: 2–3 meetings booked by end of month 1.
Month 2
Full sequence cadence running. Multi-thread activations on accounts where Tony has meetings progressing. Messaging tightened based on what's getting replies. Target: 6–8 qualified meetings booked.
Month 3+
Full partnership at capacity. Tier 1 fully penetrated, Tier 2 sequences running, multi-thread active on 3–5 deals. Target: 8–10 qualified meetings per month consistently contributing to Tony's pipeline.

The one thing that separates good SDRs from great ones: great SDRs make the AE's first call easier. The brief you send before a meeting, the research you do before touching an account, the log you keep so I can see exactly what was said — that's part of your job as much as the dialing.

The reps who get promoted fastest are the ones who make their AE look good. When I walk into a call and nail it because you gave me perfect context — that reflects on you. Own that part of the job.
